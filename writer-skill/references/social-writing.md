# Social writing: an editorial playbook

Use for Telegram blog posts, X posts/threads, and LinkedIn. These are craft heuristics, not guaranteed reach formulas. Keep the author's actual angle and the platform's current affordances ahead of a template.

## 1. Find the point before the hook

Write one private sentence: “The reader will see X differently because Y.” Locate the strongest *available* ingredient: a real contradiction, concrete fact, operator scene, mistake, cost, or personal judgment. If none exists, ask for one or make a small observation; do not fake lived experience, numbers, outcomes, quotes, or stakes.

## 2. Build the opening from what the body can pay off

Draft several different openings silently and choose one: (a) a specific surprising fact, (b) a recognizable scene, (c) a supported contrarian claim, (d) a practical question, (e) a costly mistake. The first line must already carry meaning. The next beat explains or complicates it. Avoid “вы не поверите”, “непопулярное мнение”, “это изменит всё”, and an unsupported cliffhanger. A hook is a promise, not bait.

## 3. Develop the idea without losing pace

Use one coherent throughline: what happened / what the author actually tried → the mechanism or contradiction → the consequence *if supported*. A paragraph earns its place by adding evidence, scene, explanation, interpretation, or tension. Name the actor and action. Keep facts and personal interpretation visibly separate. Retain the original author's humour, emotion, and useful roughness. Delete repeated setup and neat but empty transitions. A strong sharp claim should be followed by the specific reason, not ten adjectives.

## 4. Land an earned ending

Choose the closer that fits the material: an observation that changes how the reader sees the opening; a practical decision/fork; a question whose answer genuinely matters; or a light ironic sting rooted in the evidence. A conclusion is not necessarily a “мораль”. Avoid recap sentences, generic positivity, “согласны?”, and forced sales CTA. When a CTA has a real business purpose, make it specific and proportionate.

## 5. Adapt, don't duplicate

- **Telegram:** conversational, breathable paragraphs; mix short hits with fuller thoughts instead of one two-word line after another. Let a real scene or direct question lead. Use bullets only when compressing real items. For Evgeny's channel, respect his latest `evgeny-content-style`, `content-chat-writing-style`, `postcraft` and `tg` guidance, including copy-paste format and channel sign-off when applicable. Preserve his slight edge and jokes; do not turn posts into a dry report.
- **X:** one post should have one self-contained thought, with a hook and landing even when very short. Check current account limits before asserting a hard character ceiling; for a thread, make the first post stand alone and give each subsequent post a new step, not a split sentence. Cut throat-clearing, not the mechanism. No automatic hashtag pile.
- **LinkedIn:** an opening that works before the “see more” fold; then a concrete work scene, decision, trade-off, or evidence and an interpretation. Professional does not mean corporate sludge or invented founder struggle. A real question can end the post, but never add “Thoughts?” for engagement. Use line breaks for readability, not faux-poetry.
- If asked for all three, produce genuinely adapted versions rather than trimming a Telegram text into X and padding it for LinkedIn. User's specified language overrides platform defaults.

## Editorial pressure test

- Can a stranger understand why the first line matters without being tricked?
- Does the body actually pay the opening's promise?
- Is the sharpness attached to a supported detail rather than performance of outrage?
- Does the ending add a new angle, implication, or decision instead of repeating the opener?
- Are all facts, figures, implied benefits, first-person claims, and causal links in the source or explicitly verified?
- Would the author recognize this voice? If a small personal post has no insight thesis, don't invent one.
