# B NOVA Start Project Skill

Use this skill when starting a B NOVA client project, packaging a landing/site/SEO offer, preparing a discovery pipeline, or explaining why the work costs more than a cheap page. The core stance: B NOVA does not start with design. It starts with business meaning, audience, objections, competitors, offer logic, and only then turns that into site structure, SEO, CRM and AI workflows.

## When to Use

Use when the task involves:

- first client discovery for a B NOVA website, landing page, SEO or AI-assistant project;
- creating a project start pipeline or brief;
- packaging a 200k+ landing/site offer against cheaper 50k "just page" alternatives;
- writing a proposal block about pre-project analysis;
- creating site/landing structure from interviews, reviews, competitors and objections;
- preparing artifacts for sales, ads, content, CRM scripts or AI assistant logic.

Do not use for:

- pure visual redesign without business discovery;
- technical bug fixing;
- publishing or sending proposals without user approval;
- legal, payment, or production-side commitments.

## Core Principle

A high-ticket B NOVA website or landing is not sold as a set of screens. It is sold as a business argument system:

1. Find what clients value.
2. Capture audience objections.
3. Study competitors and reviews.
4. Build a map of meanings and sales arguments.
5. Convert that map into page structure, SEO content, ads, CRM scripts and AI assistant behavior.

Cheap version: a contractor makes a page.

B NOVA version: we extract the business value, package arguments, build the site around them, and reuse those meanings across marketing and sales.

## Discovery Pipeline

### 1. Business Context

Collect:

- business niche and geography;
- services/products with highest margin or priority;
- current lead sources;
- average check and sales cycle if available;
- who answers leads and where they are lost;
- current website, ads, CRM and messengers;
- what the client considers a successful result.

Completion criterion: the project has a clear commercial goal, not only a design request.

### 2. Audience and Value

Find why people buy:

- what problem the service solves;
- what clients fear before buying;
- what makes the company credible;
- which factors influence choice: price, speed, guarantees, location, expertise, cases, convenience, safety;
- what the client says in real language, not agency language.

Useful sources:

- client interview;
- sales manager notes;
- call/chat history if the client provides it;
- reviews of the client and competitors;
- public forums, maps, marketplaces and niche directories.

Completion criterion: there is a list of real customer pains, buying triggers and decision factors.

### 3. Objections

Create an objections list:

- expensive;
- unclear result;
- afraid of poor quality;
- do not trust contractors;
- need proof;
- need timeline;
- need examples;
- need guarantee or clear process;
- not ready to call immediately.

For each objection, define how the site closes it:

- block on the page;
- FAQ answer;
- proof/case;
- process explanation;
- pricing note;
- form microcopy;
- messenger/AI assistant response.

Completion criterion: every major objection has a corresponding site or sales asset.

### 4. Competitors and Reviews

Analyze competitors to understand:

- what they promise;
- what pages and services they have;
- which keywords and landing pages they use;
- what proof blocks they show;
- where their offer is stronger;
- where their offer is weak.

Review analysis:

- collect repeated praise points;
- collect repeated complaints;
- extract exact client language;
- identify themes that can become page blocks, ads, FAQ and sales scripts.

If enough data exists, analyze dozens or hundreds of reviews. Mention the real amount only if actually checked. Do not invent numbers like "600+ reviews" unless verified from source material.

Completion criterion: there is a competitor gap list and a set of borrowed-but-not-copied market expectations.

### 5. Map of Meanings

Create a "Карта смыслов бизнеса" as a named project artifact.

It should include:

- target audiences;
- key pains;
- objections;
- decision factors;
- competitors;
- differentiators;
- offer angles;
- proof blocks;
- FAQ topics;
- SEO page topics;
- ad messages;
- content topics;
- sales/CRM/AI assistant scripts.

Completion criterion: the map can be reused beyond the website: in ads, posts, sales scripts, onboarding and AI assistant logic.

### 6. Site / Landing Structure

Only after the meaning map, create structure:

- hero promise;
- target service/category pages;
- problem-solution-result blocks;
- proof/cases/reviews;
- process;
- pricing or starting price if appropriate;
- FAQ based on objections;
- CTA and form logic;
- SEO title/description/H1;
- CRM/analytics/AI touchpoints.

Completion criterion: every section has a job. No decorative blocks just to fill the page.

## Offer Packaging

When explaining a higher price, contrast clearly:

### Cheap page

- starts from design blocks;
- uses generic structure;
- mostly works only as a visual page;
- weakly helps ads, sales and CRM;
- does not deeply process audience objections.

### B NOVA project

- starts from market and audience research;
- turns objections and buying triggers into page logic;
- creates a map of meanings for site, ads, sales and content;
- builds SEO structure and business pages;
- connects lead capture, CRM, analytics and AI assistant scenarios where needed.

Useful client-facing phrase:

```text
Мы не начинаем с дизайна. Сначала достаем ценность бизнеса, возражения клиентов и аргументы продаж. Потом превращаем это в структуру сайта, SEO-страницы, тексты, формы, аналитику и сценарии обработки заявок.
```

## Standard Artifacts

For a serious B NOVA project, prepare some or all of these:

1. Discovery summary.
2. Audience and objection table.
3. Competitor findings.
4. Review-language extracts.
5. Map of business meanings.
6. Site or landing structure.
7. SEO page map.
8. Draft hero and CTA messages.
9. FAQ from objections.
10. CRM / AI assistant lead-processing notes.
11. Measurement plan: goals, forms, events, source tracking.

## Client-Facing Copy Blocks

### Block: Before Design

```text
Сайт не должен начинаться с дизайна. Сначала нужно понять, за что клиенты готовы платить, какие сомнения мешают заявке и чем компания отличается от конкурентов. После этого структура, тексты и дизайн начинают работать на продажу, а не просто выглядят аккуратно.
```

### Block: Map of Meanings

```text
На старте мы собираем карту смыслов бизнеса: аудитория, боли, возражения, конкуренты, сильные стороны, офферы и аргументы. Эта карта используется не только на сайте. Из нее можно делать рекламу, контент, скрипты продаж, FAQ и сценарии для AI-ассистента.
```

### Block: Why Not a Cheap Page

```text
Страница за 50 000 ₽ обычно собирается из готовых блоков. Проект B NOVA начинается раньше: с анализа аудитории, конкурентов, отзывов и продаж. Поэтому на выходе клиент получает не только сайт, а систему аргументов, которая помогает получать и обрабатывать заявки.
```

## Website / Landing Page Checklist

Before writing or approving structure, check:

- hero explains the service and result in plain business language;
- there is a direct path to the application form;
- every priority service has or can have a separate page;
- objections are closed in blocks and FAQ;
- proof is concrete: cases, examples, process, terms, conditions;
- SEO titles and descriptions include real service keywords;
- CTAs are consistent and not duplicated with different wording;
- mobile version keeps CTA visible and readable;
- forms include consent and privacy requirements;
- analytics can show source, action and lead result.

## Pitfalls

- Do not sell research as abstract strategy. Tie every finding to a page block, ad message, sales script or AI/CRM scenario.
- Do not invent metrics, review counts or competitor facts. Use placeholders or say what must be checked.
- Do not overuse internal terms like "semantic core", "clustering", "conversion architecture" in client-facing copy. Translate into business language.
- Do not start with Figma/layout before the map of meanings exists, unless the task is explicitly a quick MVP or visual draft.
- Do not promise guaranteed SEO positions or guaranteed lead volume.
- Do not expose personal data, client logs or raw chat/call transcripts in public artifacts.

## Verification

A B NOVA start-project deliverable is ready when:

- the commercial goal is explicit;
- target audience and objections are written down;
- competitor/review insights are separated from assumptions;
- the map of meanings exists or is clearly requested as the next artifact;
- page structure follows the meaning map;
- client-facing copy avoids jargon and unsupported guarantees;
- next step is concrete: interview, review analysis, structure draft, SEO map, prototype or proposal.
